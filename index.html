50/50 Privacy Policy

Effective date: November 22, 2026
Contact: praethix@gmail.com

50/50 is a chore tracker for Android, for anyone who shares housework, such as couples, roommates and families. It can also reward kids, or anyone else, with points for doing chores. This policy explains what the app stores, where it goes, and how to delete it. The short version: everything stays on your phone. The app has no internet access, no accounts, no ads and no analytics, and the developer never receives any of your data.

What the app stores

Everything you enter is saved in the app's private storage on your phone:

  • People in your household: names, whether each person is a kid, a colour, and an optional profile photo.
  • Activity log: which chore or activity each person did, when, for how long, and any notes you type.
  • Points and the Point Exchange: the rewards you set up and the trades (points spent) for each person.
  • Activity types: chore names, icons, point values and similar settings.
  • App settings: who this phone belongs to, a timer that's running, your recently used activities, and a short random code that tells your phone's activities apart from other phones'.

Profile photos are chosen with Android's photo picker, so the app only ever sees the photos you pick. Each photo is cropped and shrunk to a small square before it's saved. The app doesn't ask for access to your photo library, camera, contacts, location or any other personal data.

No internet, no tracking

The app doesn't have Android's internet permission, so it can't send anything over the internet. It asks for notifications and alarms only so that a TV or game-time timer bought in the Point Exchange can count down in the notification shade and ring when time is up. There are no ads, no analytics or crash-reporting services, no third-party SDKs that collect data, and no sign-in or accounts. The developer has no server and never receives your data.

Children's information

50/50 is made for adults who share chores and housework, such as couples, roommates and parents. Some households also use it to reward children for chores. In that case, the adult who owns the phone can add children as household members, with a name and, optionally, a photo, and a child can pick their own name on that phone to log a chore. Children don't have accounts, and the developer never receives their information. It stays on the phone where it was entered, apart from the transfers described below, which only happen when someone starts them on the phone.

When data leaves your phone

Data only leaves your phone in the three ways below, and you start each one yourself.

1. Showdown (tapping two phones together over NFC)

Showdown only runs while the Showdown screen is open on both phones and the phones are held together. The two phones then talk directly over NFC. No internet or server is involved.

  • On every tap, each phone sends the other: the phone owner's name and point totals for today, this week, this month and this year; a summary of each person on the phone (name, kid or adult, colour, number of logged activities and trades, and points available to spend); and profile photos the other phone doesn't already have. This is what the head-to-head comparison and the sync suggestions are built from. Photos that don't belong to anyone saved on the receiving phone are deleted the next time the app opens there.
  • Full records, meaning each person's logged activities (with notes) and trades, are only sent when you choose to sync people or activities. The phone that chooses shows the changes before you press Sync. The other phone shows what will change and applies it only if its owner accepts.

2. Export and import files

Household → Export writes a file containing everything listed above, including profile photos. You choose where it's saved, with Android's file picker: for example your phone's storage or a cloud drive you use. Once it's saved, it's your file, and the service you saved it to looks after it under its own privacy policy. Import reads a file you choose and merges it into the app.

3. Android backup

If backup is turned on in your phone's settings, Android may include 50/50's data (the database, settings and profile photos) in your phone's backup to your Google account, and copy it to a new phone when you switch phones. This is handled by Android and Google, not by the app or the developer, under Google's privacy policy. You can turn backup off in your phone's settings (usually Settings → System → Backup or Settings → Google → Backup).

How to delete your data

  • One activity: open it in the Log and delete it.
  • One trade: tap it in the Point Exchange to refund it.
  • An activity type: open it in Household → Activities & points and choose Delete activity. This also deletes every activity logged with it.
  • A person: open them in Household and choose Remove. This deletes the person together with their photo, logged activities, trades and any running timers. The phone's owner can't be removed until someone else is made the owner.
  • Everything: uninstall the app, or clear its storage in Settings → Apps → 50/50 → Storage & cache → Clear storage. This deletes all of the app's data on that phone.

Copies made outside the app are not removed automatically: files you exported, data synced to another phone (delete it there the same way), and Android backups in your Google account (manage or delete these in your Google account's backup settings).

Security

The app's data is stored in its private storage, which other apps can't read. The app has no internet connection, so its data can't be reached over the network. Export files are not encrypted, so keep them somewhere you trust.
